How to Clean and Care for New Kitchen Cabinets So They Stay Like New

New cabinets change the whole feel of a kitchen. Then life happens: cooking splatter, sticky fingerprints, a drip of coffee down a drawer front. Within a few months, the doors near the stove start to feel a little tacky, and the ones by the trash can have mystery smudges.

At North Columbus Cleaning, kitchens are a big part of every visit. We've seen how quickly cabinets show wear when they're cleaned with the wrong product, and how well they hold up when they're cared for gently and regularly. Here's what works.

Start With the Manufacturer's Instructions

Before anything else, find the care guide that came with your cabinets, or ask the company that installed them. Finishes vary a lot. A recommendation that's perfect for one finish can damage another. When in doubt, the manufacturer's guidance wins.

The tips below are general practices that work for most common finishes.

Know Your Finish

Painted cabinets are popular and look great, but paint can chip at edges and show grime around handles. They need gentle cleaners and soft cloths.

Stained wood cabinets show the grain of the wood. They're typically sealed with a clear finish. Harsh cleaners can dull or strip that finish.

Laminate and thermofoil have a smooth, manufactured surface. They're easy to wipe but can be damaged by heat and harsh chemicals.

High-gloss finishes show every fingerprint and scratch. Microfiber is your friend.

The Everyday Wipe-Down

For most cabinets, the best daily or weekly cleaner is the simplest one:

  • Warm water with a few drops of mild dish soap
  • A soft microfiber cloth, wrung out well
  • A second dry cloth to dry immediately

Wipe in the direction of the grain on wood. Pay extra attention to the areas people touch most: around handles and knobs, the bottom edge of upper cabinets and the top edge of lower drawers.

Dry right away. Water left sitting on cabinet doors, especially at seams and edges, is one of the most common causes of damage.

Grease Near the Stove

The cabinets next to and above the range collect a film of cooking grease that attracts dust and turns sticky.

  • Run the range hood every time you cook. It's the best prevention.
  • Wipe these cabinets more often than the rest of the kitchen, maybe weekly.
  • Use a little more dish soap in warm water for greasy buildup. Dish soap is made to cut grease and is usually gentle on finishes.
  • Work in small sections and dry as you go.

If buildup has gotten heavy, some people use a paste of baking soda and water, applied gently with a soft cloth. Test it in a hidden spot first, since even mild abrasives can dull some finishes.

What to Avoid

These are the habits we see cause the most damage:

  • Abrasive sponges and scrub pads. They scratch paint and dull clear finishes.
  • Harsh or ammonia-based cleaners, bleach and strong degreasers, unless your manufacturer approves them.
  • All-purpose sprays used undiluted on painted finishes.
  • Soaking-wet cloths. Excess water seeps into joints.
  • Steam cleaners on cabinet doors.
  • Hanging damp dish towels over cabinet doors. Constant moisture can damage the finish and the wood behind it.
  • Silicone-based furniture polish buildup, which can make finishes cloudy and affect future refinishing.

Hardware

Knobs and pulls get touched constantly.

  • Wipe them with the same soapy cloth during regular cleaning.
  • For a deeper clean, unscrew them and wash separately in warm, soapy water.
  • Avoid harsh metal polishes unless they're made for your specific finish, like brushed nickel or matte black.
  • Dry thoroughly before reinstalling.

Inside the Cabinets

Interiors get forgotten until something spills.

  • Empty one cabinet at a time a few times a year.
  • Vacuum crumbs with a brush attachment.
  • Wipe shelves with warm soapy water and dry.
  • Use shelf liners in cabinets that hold oils, spices or cleaning products.
  • Under the sink, check for leaks or moisture while you're there. Catching a small drip early protects the cabinet base.

Kick Plates, Toe Kicks and Crown Molding

Two spots collect dust and grime that most people never notice:

  • Toe kicks at floor level get scuffed by shoes, mop water and pet hair. Wipe them when you mop.
  • Tops of upper cabinets and crown molding collect greasy dust. Use a step stool and clean them every few months.

Glass Doors and Open Shelving

Glass-front cabinets need a glass cleaner on the glass only. Spray onto the cloth, not the door, so it doesn't drip onto the surrounding finish. Open shelving collects dust fast; a quick weekly dusting keeps it looking intentional instead of neglected.

Touch-Ups and Chips

Minor chips in painted cabinets are common at edges and corners. Many cabinet makers offer matching touch-up paint or markers. Ask for them when cabinets are installed; it's much easier than trying to match a color later.

A Simple Cabinet Cleaning Schedule

  • Daily: Wipe up splashes and spills as they happen.
  • Weekly: Wipe handles and high-touch areas. Clean cabinets near the stove.
  • Monthly: Wipe all cabinet fronts. Clean hardware.
  • Every few months: Clean tops of upper cabinets, toe kicks and interiors.
  • Yearly: Empty and clean every cabinet. Check hinges and adjust if doors are sagging.

Planning New Cabinets?

If you're still in the planning stage, it's worth thinking about cleaning when you choose finishes. Ask about how a finish holds up to daily wiping, whether touch-up kits are available, and what cleaners are approved. A good designer working on custom kitchen cabinets in Columbus can help you weigh appearance against upkeep for the way your household actually cooks.

The Bottom Line

New cabinets don't need fancy products. They need gentle, regular cleaning: mild soap, warm water, soft cloths and immediate drying. Keep up with the grease near the stove, avoid harsh cleaners and wet towels, and clean the spots people forget. Your kitchen will look newer for a lot longer.
