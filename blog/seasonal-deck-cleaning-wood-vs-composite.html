Seasonal Deck Cleaning: What Works for Wood and What Works for Composite

A deck is basically an outdoor room. It gets furniture, food, foot traffic, pets and weather all at once. And like any room, it looks a lot better when it's cleaned on a schedule instead of only when it's embarrassing.

At North Columbus Cleaning, we're usually inside the house, but our clients' back doors open onto decks all over the north side. Here's a seasonal plan for keeping a deck clean, with separate notes for wood and composite boards, because they really don't want the same treatment.

First: Know Which Deck You Have

Wood decks are usually pressure-treated pine, or sometimes cedar or another species. They absorb water, can splinter, and typically need stain or sealer every few years.

Composite decks are made from a blend of wood fibers and plastic, often with a protective outer shell. They don't need staining, but they're not maintenance-free. Dirt, pollen, mildew and grease still collect on the surface.

If you're not sure, check old paperwork or look at a cut end of a board. Composite looks uniform all the way through, or has a distinct outer cap layer.

Spring: The Big Reset

Spring is the main cleaning of the year. Winter leaves behind debris, grime and sometimes mildew, and then pollen season arrives on top of it.

For both types

  • Clear everything off: furniture, grill, planters, rugs.
  • Sweep thoroughly, including corners and along the house wall.
  • Clean the gaps between boards with a putty knife or a thin deck-gap tool. Packed debris holds moisture.
  • Check under planters and rugs for stains or trapped moisture.

For wood

  • Wash with a cleaner made for wood decks, or an oxygen bleach solution, using a stiff nylon brush. Follow label directions.
  • Scrub with the grain.
  • Rinse well and let the deck dry completely, often a couple of days.
  • Inspect for raised nails or screws, soft spots and splinters.
  • If water no longer beads on the surface, it may be time to re-stain or seal.

For composite

  • Wash with warm water and mild dish soap, or a cleaner your board manufacturer approves.
  • Use a soft-bristle brush. Stiff brushes can scratch some surfaces.
  • Rinse thoroughly so soap doesn't leave a film.
  • Check the manufacturer's care guide before using any bleach or strong cleaner. Many brands publish specific guidance.

About pressure washers

Pressure washers can gouge wood and raise fuzzy fibers. On composite, many manufacturers either advise against them or set limits on pressure and nozzle distance, and using one improperly may affect a warranty. A brush and hose are safer for most decks.

Summer: Keep Up With Use

Summer is when the deck gets the most use and the most mess.

  • Sweep weekly. Grass clippings, pollen and blossoms are easier to remove before they get wet.
  • Clean spills right away. Food, wine, sunscreen and bug spray can stain both wood and composite.
  • Watch the grill area. Grease is the most common deck stain. Use a grill mat underneath, and wipe drips quickly with warm soapy water.
  • Move planters and furniture occasionally so you don't get pale or dark rectangles.
  • Rinse after pollen bursts or big storms.

Mildew

Central Ohio summers are humid, and shady decks grow mildew fast. You'll see it as gray, green or black spots. On wood, a deck cleaner or oxygen bleach solution usually handles it. On composite, use the cleaner recommended by the manufacturer. Better airflow, like trimming back shrubs, helps prevent it from coming back.

Fall: Get Ahead of the Leaves

Fall is short but important.

  • Remove leaves often. Wet leaves left on a deck can stain boards, especially wood, and they hold moisture against the surface.
  • Clear the gaps again before winter so water can drain.
  • Clean furniture before storing it so grime doesn't set over the winter.
  • Do a light wash in early fall, before it gets too cold for things to dry.
  • Check gutters near the deck. Overflowing gutters can dump water and debris right onto the boards.

Winter: Gentle Is Better

Snow on a deck isn't a problem by itself, but how you remove it can be.

  • Use a plastic shovel, and push along the length of the boards, not across them.
  • Leave a thin layer rather than scraping to bare boards. Scraping is where scratches happen.
  • Be careful with ice melt. Some products can damage wood or discolor composite. Check your decking manufacturer's guidance. Calcium chloride is commonly listed as acceptable for many composite brands, but verify for yours.
  • Never use metal tools or chip ice off with a shovel edge.

Don't Forget the Inside of the Door

The deck's dirt doesn't stay outside. A few indoor habits help:

  • Put a washable mat inside the deck door, plus a coarse mat outside.
  • Vacuum the door track regularly. It collects sand, leaves and pet hair.
  • Clean the glass on both sides in spring and fall.
  • Wipe the interior floor near the door weekly in summer.

When we clean homes with busy decks, the floor within a few feet of the door is almost always the dirtiest spot in the kitchen.

When It's Time for More Than Cleaning

Cleaning keeps a deck looking good, but it can't fix structural issues. Call a deck professional if you notice:

  • Soft, spongy or rotting boards
  • Loose or wobbly railings
  • Posts or ledger boards that show rot or movement
  • Boards that are badly cupped, cracked or splintered
  • Stairs that feel unstable

If you have an aging wood deck and you're tired of re-staining, some homeowners look into composite decks in Columbus as a lower-maintenance option. It's worth talking with a contractor about what makes sense for your yard and budget.

A Quick Seasonal Checklist

Spring: Clear, sweep, clean gaps, wash, inspect, re-seal wood if needed.

Summer: Sweep weekly, clean spills fast, protect the grill zone, treat mildew.

Fall: Remove leaves often, clear gaps, light wash, store clean furniture.

Winter: Plastic shovel, push with the boards, use deck-safe ice melt.

The Bottom Line

Wood and composite decks both need regular cleaning; they just need slightly different tools and products. Follow the seasons, keep the gaps clear, deal with grease and leaves quickly, and always check the manufacturer's advice before reaching for strong cleaners or a pressure washer. A clean deck lasts longer and keeps a lot more dirt out of your kitchen.
